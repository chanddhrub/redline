# Agent 2: What Goes Wrong — Clause Types That Actually Burn People

Research question: which recurring clause types show up most often in real complaints,
enforcement actions, and disputes (across consumer contracts, employment, leases, and
freelance/gig agreements)? Ranked by evidentiary weight, not intuition.

**Method:** targeted web search against regulator (FTC, CFPB), state AG, legal-industry
survey, and nonprofit/advocacy sources. 8 web searches used, 2 page-fetch attempts (both
failed — paywall/redirect — so all findings below rest on search-result snippets from
primary or near-primary sources, not full-page reads).

## Detail per finding

**1. Auto-renewal / negative option.** This is the most directly regulator-documented
category: the FTC's own rulemaking record states thousands of complaints per year from
across all 50 states, and current enforcement (e.g., against Uber for Uber One
enrollment/cancellation practices) is active and well-covered as of early 2026, even
after the Eighth Circuit vacated the 2024 rule amendments on procedural grounds. The
underlying Section 5/ROSCA enforcement authority survives the court loss.

**2. Mandatory arbitration + class-action waiver.** Less "complaint volume" evidence and
more "structural prevalence + harm" evidence: the clause is nearly universal among large
companies (81/100 of the largest US firms per a 2019 UC Davis Law Review study cited in
search results) and consumers are systematically unaware they're bound by it (99% per
NCLC). This is a clause people don't know they signed until they need to sue and can't.

**3. Limitation of liability / indemnification.** This ranks by negotiation frequency
in B2B/commercial contracts (WorldCC's survey, described secondhand — I did not read the
primary WorldCC report), not consumer complaint volume. Include it because Redline's
target set includes freelance/service contracts where this is the single most fought-over
clause economically, even if it doesn't generate regulatory complaints the way consumer
subscription traps do.

**4. Late fees / junk fees.** Strong CFPB documentation: dollar figures ($12B→$14.5B
late fees 2020→2022), a specific rule targeting $10B+/year in fee reduction, and a
measured 66% rise in bank-fee complaints 2021–2023. This is one of the best-quantified
categories in this research.

**5. Non-competes.** Evidence here is comment-volume, not lawsuit/complaint-count data —
26,000+ public comments on the FTC's proposed rule (>25,000 supportive), with the FTC's
own fact sheet citing real worker-harm case studies drawn from media reports. The rule
was later vacated, so this is documented public concern rather than a current enforcement
statistic.

**6. Security deposits.** Only one state's data found (New York): ~5,000 AG complaints
since 2023, ~$2.1M recovered. Did not find comparable multi-state or national
aggregation in the searches run — flagging this as a gap.

**7–8. Freelance/scope and payment clauses.** Sourced from a single secondary blog
aggregating what it says is Freelancers Union survey data (60–70% of disputes tied to
scope-of-work ambiguity; 71% of freelancers struggled to collect payment; 28% contract
usage rate; 73% dispute reduction with written contracts). I could not verify this
against the primary Freelancers Union report directly — treat these numbers as
lower-confidence pending primary-source confirmation.

## What I could not find

- No comprehensive, cross-category ranking (i.e., a single source ranking auto-renewal
  vs. arbitration vs. non-compete vs. indemnification by complaint volume) — this
  report's overall ranking is my synthesis across disparate evidence types (regulatory
  complaint counts, rulemaking comment volume, industry negotiation surveys, and one
  secondary-sourced freelance survey), not a single measured ranking. Treat rank order
  as impressionistic beyond roughly the top 2–4 slots, which have the strongest direct
  regulator documentation.
- Could not fetch the WorldCC "Most Negotiated Terms" report directly (only read
  secondary summaries of it) — the primary report likely has better-sourced clause
  rankings for B2B/commercial contracts and would strengthen rank 3.
- Could not find national (vs. single-state) security-deposit complaint statistics.
- Did not find CFPB/FTC data specifically isolating "unilateral amendment" ("we may
  change these terms at any time") or "personal guarantee" or "exclusivity" clauses as
  distinct complaint categories — these appeared in background research as commonly
  flagged contract-review concerns but I found no complaint-volume or enforcement data
  for them specifically within the search budget. Not included in the ranked table for
  lack of sourcing, per the hard guardrail against speculation.
- Did not investigate IP-assignment clauses, joint-and-several liability, or holdover
  rent specifically — ran out of search budget (8/12 searches used, stopped once 8
  distinct sourced findings were reached, per instructions) before targeting these.
- Two WebFetch attempts (EPI page, Gothamist article) both failed (403 / redirect not
  followed) — findings 2 and 6 rest on search-snippet text rather than confirmed
  full-page primary content.
